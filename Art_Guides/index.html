Go to the apps

How these apps came to be

In my regular work as an astronomy professor and academic leader, I have been steeped in the exacting analysis of stars, galaxies and the universe. I have also spent many years studying another kind of universe, the world of higher education with all of its forces and dynamics.

To cross-train my mind and tap into other ways of knowing, I set out to develop my dormant artistic abilities. Art was a central part of who I was in high school, but I shelved it to pursue my career. Coming back to it has meant enrolling in classes at Laguna College of Art and Design and at Saddleback College, where I have been working to build my skills in drawing and painting.

The process has had many unexpected benefits, and the biggest one is that it has taught me to see differently. A yoga instructor once told me that “if you’re breathing, you’re doing yoga.” In the same spirit, I believe that if you’re seeing, you’re doing art. Seeing color through painting, and seeing the underlying structure of the human body through figure drawing, has enriched my life very deeply.

Being a student again has also taught me things I bring back to my work as a professor and educational leader. Facing a difficult challenge and struggling in a new field is an invaluable experience for anyone who teaches, and it has pushed me toward a more compassionate and effective approach to teaching. I have also found that cultivating an artistic side is part of a more general process, an ongoing dialog between seeing and knowing.

To help others on the same journey, I have built a set of apps for drawing the human figure. They are below, and they are still being tested so I can refine them and make them more useful for people like me who are new to figure drawing. Enjoy!

The apps

They run in your browser on a computer, tablet or phone. Nothing to install.

  • Start here

    Loomis Head Rotator

    Build the classic Loomis head step by step, from the sliced ball and jaw to the features. Turn it to any angle and move the light to study the shadows.

    Open the app
  • Portraits

    Portrait Head Mapper

    Place a few dots on a photo of a face and the app wraps it onto a construction head. Then turn the person to any angle as a reference for a portrait.

    Open the app
  • Full figure

    Figure Builder

    Build a posable figure from the pelvis and rib cage out to a muscled mannequin. Pick a pose, turn it, zoom in, and see the structure under the skin.

    Open the app
  • Advanced

    Buildable Head

    A finished head with sculpted eyes, nose, mouth and ears. Reshape the features and the head, or let the app fit it to a photo of a real face.

    Open the app
  • Advanced figure

    Advanced Figure Builder

    A realistic man and woman you can pose, place in front of your own background picture, and light from any direction. Push the light brighter and the shadows darker, then switch to two or three values to see exactly where light turns to shadow. A muscle map drawn from my class charts can color and label the muscle groups.

    Open the app

Photos you use in the portrait apps, and background pictures, stay on your own device. Each app can save your settings so you can pick up where you left off.
